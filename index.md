## Research identity

Rather than treating GIS, AI or environmental science as separate toolboxes, I work toward a single research language for **spatially explicit environmental systems**. The central question is how to represent places, processes, people, documents and decisions in computational models that remain geographically grounded, scientifically auditable and operationally useful.

My work is organized around five connected domains:

  <div class="card premium"><div class="eyebrow">01 · Geoinformatics</div><h3>Spatial data as a computational substrate</h3><p>GIS, remote sensing, spatial databases, geostatistics, geospatial data engineering and reproducible spatial analytics for environmental and regional systems.</p></div>
  <div class="card premium"><div class="eyebrow">02 · Ecological engineering simulation</div><h3>Modeling coupled human–environment systems</h3><p>Environmental modeling, ecological process abstraction, risk simulation, scenario analysis, livelihood–resource interactions and decision-oriented digital representations.</p></div>
  <div class="card premium"><div class="eyebrow">03 · Computational social science</div><h3>People, behavior and local knowledge in space</h3><p>Spatial indicators, social-environmental data, community knowledge, agent-based modeling and place-based interpretation of adaptation and resource-use behavior.</p></div>
  <div class="card premium"><div class="eyebrow">04 · WebGeo / WebGIS / GeoNLP</div><h3>From browser-native maps to geographic language intelligence</h3><p>Web geospatial systems, spatial APIs, geocoding, geographic entity extraction, place-aware text mining, environmental corpora and spatial-textual knowledge graphs.</p></div>
