# Geoinformatics for Environmental & Social Systems

My research program studies how **space can become an executable layer of intelligence**: not only a map on which observations are displayed, but a computational substrate for environmental models, social processes, language evidence and AI agents.

<div class="signature-box">
  <div class="signature-main">GEODATA → MODELS → LANGUAGE → AGENTS → DECISIONS</div>
  <p>The unifying objective is to create spatially explicit, evidence-grounded and reproducible systems that connect environmental processes with human behavior and machine reasoning.</p>
</div>

<div class="research-grid">

  <div class="card premium"><div class="eyebrow">Pillar 06</div><h3>GeoAI & spatial agents</h3><p>Machine learning and LLM-based systems that reason with geographic context, maps, tools, layers, constraints and memory. ## Spatial-agent architecture

A spatial agent is not defined here as a chatbot with a map attached. It is an agent whose **state, memory, tools and decisions are constrained by geographic structure**.

<div class="flowline">
  <span>Observe</span><b>→</b><span>Locate</span><b>→</b><span>Retrieve</span><b>→</b><span>Model</span><b>→</b><span>Plan</span><b>→</b><span>Act / Recommend</span><b>→</b><span>Audit</span>
</div>

A research-grade implementation should therefore expose at least five explicit objects: **place**, **layer**, **event**, **evidence** and **action**. This enables geographic provenance to be traced through a complete reasoning cycle instead of being lost inside natural-language output.

## Environmental simulation as a coupled system

The ecological-engineering branch of the program models interactions among:

- environmental drivers: salinity, flooding, sea-level rise, water conditions and land-use change;
- ecological/resource states: mangroves, aquaculture areas, agricultural systems and coastal resources;
- human agents: households, livelihood groups, managers and local institutions;
- information flows: reports, monitoring data, local knowledge and policy documents;
- interventions: adaptation, land-use decisions, production conversion and resource-management scenarios.

This framing extends the LLKH foundation in coastal livelihoods, climate-change adaptation and GIS toward **coupled spatial simulation and decision intelligence**.

## Geographic laboratory

The strategic geographic focus remains the **Vietnamese Mekong Delta coastal system**, especially Ben Tre, Tra Vinh, coastal Vinh Long, Can Gio and Kien Giang, with earlier island/coastal work including Ly Son. These places provide linked environmental and social systems for testing salinity adaptation, land-use transition, ecological livelihoods, aquaculture, mangrove conservation and policy evidence.

## Research standard

The target is not maximum model complexity. The target is a research program with the characteristics expected of internationally competitive work:

- explicit research questions and falsifiable assumptions;
- reusable data models and open geospatial formats;
- reproducible experiments and documented simulation parameters;
- spatially grounded evaluation rather than generic AI benchmarks alone;
- field/expert validation and human-in-the-loop checking;
- strong separation between evidence, model inference and policy recommendation;
- datasets, code, diagrams and evaluation protocols that can be reused by other researchers.
