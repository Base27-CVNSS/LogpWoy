# Research

The research program is organized around a single question: <strong>How can geographic evidence, environmental processes, social behavior, and spatial language be integrated into computational systems that remain interpretable, reproducible, and useful for real decisions?</strong>

## Intellectual lineage

This profile uses a conceptual lineage that begins with Russian natural zonality, landscape, and territorial-regionalization traditions; continues through Chinese natural regionalization and geoinformatics development; and is localized through Vietnamese physical geography, GIS, remote sensing, and applied geoinformatics.

The lineage is presented as an <strong>influence map</strong>, not a formal adviser–student genealogy.

## Design principles

- <strong>Evidence first:</strong> every computational output should remain traceable to data, assumptions, and model steps.
- <strong>Spatial grounding:</strong> language and AI outputs should be connected to explicit locations, scales, and geographic relationships.
- <strong>Reproducibility:</strong> workflows should be executable, inspectable, and portable across desktop and web environments.
- <strong>Human-in-the-loop:</strong> expert judgment and local knowledge remain part of the modeling system rather than being treated as noise.
- <strong>Decision relevance:</strong> technical sophistication is useful only when it improves interpretation, comparison, or action.
