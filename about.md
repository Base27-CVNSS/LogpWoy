# A Manifesto for Pursuing Geoinformatics

<div class="signature-box">
  <div class="signature-main">GEOGRAPHY AS FOUNDATION · COMPUTING AS INSTRUMENT · PEOPLE AT THE CENTER</div>
  <p>I pursue Geoinformatics as a long-term intellectual path: to understand the Earth geographically, to model it computationally, and to transform evidence into human-centered decisions for a more sustainable future.</p>
</div>

## The path I choose

I choose <strong>Geoinformatics</strong> not because it is the safest path, but because it is the field in which the two strongest parts of my experience can operate as one: <strong>more than two decades of geographic thinking</strong> and years of close engagement in an <strong>Engineering Technology</strong> environment where GIS, BIM, computing, modeling, and spatial information systems intersect.

Many people are asked to choose between disciplines. I have come to believe that my strongest position is precisely at the intersection. Geography explains <em>where</em>, <em>why there</em>, <em>at what scale</em>, and <em>through which relationships</em>. Computing makes those questions operational through algorithms, data structures, simulation, programming, and artificial intelligence.

<div class="flowline">
  <span>GEOGRAPHY</span><b>→</b><span>SPATIAL DATA</span><b>→</b><span>COMPUTATION</span><b>→</b><span>MODELING & AI</span><b>→</b><span>HUMAN-CENTERED DECISIONS</span>
</div>

For me, this is not a compromise between Geography and Computer Science. It is a deliberate synthesis: <strong>geographic theory guides the questions; computational methods expand what can be measured, simulated, predicted, and explained.</strong>

## Why Geoinformatics matters

Modern challenges such as <strong>climate change, resource management, livelihood transition, urban transformation, renewable-energy development, and resilient infrastructure</strong> cannot be understood from one side alone. They require the ability to connect spatial evidence with process, behavior, technology, and policy.

That means being able to work across GIS and remote sensing, spatial statistics, databases, web technologies, social and environmental data, simulation, and AI — while still understanding the geographical meaning behind every result. The technical toolkit I continue to deepen includes <strong>C/C++, JavaScript, Python, R, Go, and Rust</strong>, together with GIS, BIM, WebGIS, spatial analysis, agent-based modeling, and modern AI methods.

I do not want to become a geographer who stops at description, nor a technologist who can build systems without understanding the spatial processes they represent. I aim to operate as a <strong>bridge between geographic reasoning and computational engineering</strong>.

## The niche I intend to build

I therefore remain firmly committed to Geoinformatics. I do not see value in abandoning it for either pure Geography or pure information technology. Doing so would separate capabilities that are most useful when combined.

Instead, I intend to deepen a focused niche in <strong>intelligent spatial systems</strong>, particularly:

- <strong>Simulation:</strong> agent-based modeling, cellular automata, system dynamics, landscape and socio-environmental dynamics.
- <strong>GeoAI:</strong> spatial machine learning, graph learning, prediction, optimization, and interpretable geographic AI.
- <strong>GeoNLP:</strong> geographic language understanding, place-aware corpora, spatial ontologies, knowledge graphs, and retrieval-augmented reasoning.
- <strong>Spatial agents:</strong> AI agents that can reason with maps, query spatial databases, operate geospatial tools, compare scenarios, and explain decisions.
- <strong>Human behavior & urban systems:</strong> GIS-integrated ABM, social-network analysis, social media, urban analytics, and spatial decision support.
